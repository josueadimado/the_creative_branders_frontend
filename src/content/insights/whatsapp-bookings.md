A lot of small businesses in Ghana and Nigeria already run on WhatsApp. Customers message to ask about prices, opening hours and whether there's a slot tomorrow. The trouble is that most of those messages arrive in the evening, when nobody is watching the phone, and by the morning the customer has booked somewhere else.

A WhatsApp assistant answers those messages for you. It uses the WhatsApp Business Platform, the official version of WhatsApp for companies, and connects to your booking calendar. When someone asks for an appointment, it offers the times you actually have free, takes the booking and sends a confirmation. The next day it can send a reminder, which on its own cuts down on missed appointments.

It's worth being clear about what it shouldn't do. An assistant should say that it's automated. It should hand the conversation to a person when a customer is upset, has a complicated question, or asks for someone by name. And it should only answer from information you've approved, such as your services, prices and policies.

Setting one up usually takes a few weeks. Most of that time goes into agreeing the questions it should answer and connecting it to your calendar. The technology is the quick part. You'll also need a WhatsApp Business account in your company's name and a phone number that isn't used on a personal phone.

It's a good fit if you get the same questions every day, take bookings or orders, or lose customers because replies are slow. If most of your messages are long, one-off conversations, a person will still do that better.

We built a similar assistant for Relief Chiropractic in Michigan, where callers book appointments by phone and the booking goes straight into the clinic's schedule. The same approach works on WhatsApp.
